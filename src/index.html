Book I · The Call to Adventure
✦   ✦   ✦

Every odyssey begins
with a question.

What can I build if I keep learning?

My journey into software engineering began with curiosity: the desire to understand how the digital world works, how a few lines of logic can become something useful, and how technology can turn an idea into an experience someone can actually use.

What began as exploration grew into a practice of building, experimenting, researching, writing, and collaborating. I have learned that becoming an engineer is not a straight road. It is a voyage shaped by unfamiliar tools, difficult problems, unexpected discoveries, and the willingness to begin again.

Troy · The First Lines

Every journey has a point of departure. Mine was the decision to move beyond simply using technology and begin understanding how it is made: learning programming fundamentals, exploring Python, and discovering the satisfaction of solving a problem step by step.

The Lotus-Eaters · The Comfort of the Familiar

It is easy to remain with what already works. Growth asks me to leave the familiar behind: to try another framework, read unfamiliar documentation, experiment with a new tool, and resist the temptation to stop at the first working version.

Learning requires exploration
IIITHE TEST
⟁

The Cyclops · Debugging the Unknown

Some problems look enormous until they are broken into smaller ones. Debugging has taught me to inspect assumptions, trace errors, test carefully, and understand why a solution works—not merely how to make an error disappear.

Precision over guesswork
IVTHE WINDS
〰

Aeolus · Learning to Navigate

Projects rarely follow a perfectly predictable route. Choosing an architecture, managing dependencies, integrating services, and deciding what to build next all require judgment. I am learning to plan carefully while staying adaptable when the wind changes.

Structure meets adaptability
VTHE DEEP
⌁

Scylla & Charybdis · Difficult Trade-offs

Engineering means balancing competing priorities: simplicity and flexibility, performance and maintainability, ambitious features and realistic scope. There is rarely one perfect answer. The work is learning to make thoughtful decisions and explain them.

Trade-offs are part of the craft

The Sirens · Focus Amid Distraction

New technologies and exciting ideas are everywhere. The challenge is to stay anchored to the problem at hand: finish meaningful work, document decisions, test what I create, and avoid mistaking endless experimentation for progress.

Intentional work over noise

The Crew · Collaboration & Community

Technology is a voyage best shared.

Engineering is not only an individual pursuit. Through collaborative projects, leadership, and nonprofit work, I have learned that building technology also means listening, communicating, organizing, and making room for other people to contribute.

As a co-founder of Classroom Crafted, I help build an educational initiative designed to make learning in mathematics, computer science, and CAD more accessible to younger students through free virtual instruction and guided projects.

I am also developing FreshStart Connect, a project concept focused on helping freshmen connect with upperclassmen through shared interests, goals, and support needs. These experiences remind me that the most meaningful technology begins with understanding people.

05
The Lessons of the Sea · Principles

What the voyage has taught me

The tools will change. The habits that make good engineers will continue to matter.

01

Stay curious.

Ask why, investigate how, and keep learning beyond the boundaries of a single language or framework.

02

Build with purpose.

Start with a real problem and let the needs of the people using a product guide the work.

03

Welcome the difficult parts.

Errors, revisions, and unexpected constraints are not detours from engineering. They are part of it.

04

Share what you learn.

Document the process, explain the discoveries, and help make technical knowledge more accessible.

Ithaca is not

There are still languages to learn, systems to understand, projects to build, and questions I have not yet thought to ask. That is what makes the journey worth continuing.
